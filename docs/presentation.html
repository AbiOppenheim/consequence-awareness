Does fiction jailbreak a model by changing what it thinks is real?

Fiction and role-play framings reliably defeat model refusals. The standard account says they suppress a refusal direction. We test a different mechanism: that they shift an upstream representation of whether the situation is real — and that refusal is conditioned on that.

Part one

The object we are studying

Before any experiment: what is actually inside a language model that you could measure, and why is it measurable with tools you already know? Everything in this part is standard background — no results yet.

Background

The residual stream: a running summary you can read and edit

This is the one object you need. The name is worse than the thing.

A decoder-only transformer keeps one vector per token position, of dimension d_model (3584 here). Call it h. The model is a stack of 28 blocks, and every block does exactly this: 

h ← h + attention(h)    # attention reads h, computes, ADDS its result back
h ← h + mlp(h)          # the MLP does the same

That is the residual stream: a per-token accumulator. It starts as the token's embedding, and each block adds into it. No block overwrites it.

It is a running summary

The vector after block ℓ is "everything the model has worked out using the first ℓ blocks." Early layers hold lexical and syntactic information; later layers hold task-level meaning.

Additive ⇒ separable

If sixteen attention heads each add a vector, the result is their sum. You can ask "how much did this component push in direction v?" — incoherent in a network where layers replaced their input.

Additive ⇒ editable

h ← h + α·v is a well-formed edit that composes with what the model was already doing. This is the entire reason steering works — and why "the stream gets rewritten 28 times" is a damaging way to picture it.

The analogy that holds — and where it breaks

Holds: the residual stream at one layer and token is a contextual embedding — one vector per prompt from a frozen feature extractor. Everything after Stage 01 is ordinary linear algebra and scikit-learn on those embeddings.

Breaks: unlike a word embedding, this vector is mid-computation. The model is going to keep using it. That is what makes intervention possible — and it is why a direction can be perfectly readable at a layer where editing it does nothing, which is one of this project's actual findings.

Background

Concepts as directions, not coordinates

You already know this result from word embeddings: king − man + woman ≈ queen. It says "royalty" and "gender" are directions, findable by arithmetic on class means, and movable along.

The linear representation hypothesis relocates that claim: the same is true of activations inside a running transformer. Sentiment, truth, refusal, formality — encoded as linear directions, extractable the same way.

This project takes that as a working assumption and tests one concept: does the model represent a situation as real or hypothetical, and is that what its refusal behaviour is conditioned on?

What we extract, concretely

acts : [n_prompts, 28, 3584]  float32
        ↑          ↑     ↑
        prompt   layer  d_model

One 3584-dimensional feature vector per prompt per layer, at one token position: the last token of the prompt, right before generation would begin.

Why that position? In a decoder-only model the next token is produced from the residual stream at the final position. Whatever the model has decided about how to respond is legible there, at the moment it decides. Averaging over the prompt would measure something real but different, and would no longer line up with the refusal decision.

The position is fixed and identical for all four directions in this project. That is what makes their cosine similarities comparable at all.

Design

Two claims, and a rule about how to treat them

Claim 1 — Existence & distinctness

Is real-vs-hypothetical linearly decodable? Does it generalize to framing templates never seen in training? And is it a different axis from the two known rivals — r̂ (refusal, Arditi et al. 2024) and v_MP (compliant persona, Zhong & Li 2026)?

Claim 2 — Causality

Does adding α·v_C to the residual stream during a fiction-framed attack restore refusal — beyond what a random direction, and beyond what any other extracted direction, explains?

The rule that governs the whole project

Every outcome is a publishable result.

  • v_C exists, is distinct, steering works → a new causal defense target.
  • v_C is just v_MP relabelled → a useful negative result.
  • v_C exists but steering does nothing → a correlate, not a cause; defense work stays on refusal.
  • v_C doesn't generalize to held-out templates → the probe learned surface tokens. Say so.

Never optimize toward a positive result. The moment you propose a change that would make the result "work", you have stopped running the experiment. Two of the findings below are negatives, and they are reported at the same volume as the positives.

Why this is worth doing at all

A 2026 interpretability agenda — From Adversarial Poetry to Adversarial Tales — proposes exactly this probe-and-steer test for narrative jailbreaks, and runs no experiments. This is its first empirical check.

A correction to the standard framing

It is often said that Arditi et al. showed jailbreaks work by suppressing the refusal direction. What that paper actually shows (§5.1) is suppression for GCG-style adversarial suffixes, on Qwen-1.8B-Chat, measured as cosine similarity of last-token activations — correlational, one attack family, and not fiction framing. Scoping the claim correctly strengthens the case for running this experiment.

Part two

Building the instruments

Six stages that produce a measured direction and establish it is real, distinct, and not an artifact of wording. None of this is causal evidence yet — and the design spends most of its effort making sure we would notice if it were wrong.

Stage 00 the contrast dataset CPU

The only thing we author from scratch

Everything downstream inherits this dataset's flaws. It is the single biggest determinant of whether the result means anything.

The design problem

To find a direction for a binary concept, collect activations for many examples of each side and subtract the class means. So we need prompt pairs that differ only in that concept.

"Only" is doing enormous work. If real-framed prompts are longer, we extract a length direction. Different vocabulary → a vocabulary direction. Different topics → a topic direction. Each would produce a beautiful, highly significant, completely worthless result.

Held-out templates, never held-out examples

Split 2,000 rows randomly and the test set contains rows built from templates seen in training. A classifier then scores 0.99 by memorizing that "hypothetically" means class 1. It has learned the wording, not the concept — and you cannot tell from the number.

So: 100 training templates, 40 held out, no overlap. Then one level stricter — routes (the mechanism a template uses) are also disjoint, 31 vs 20.

Matched task content, verified

Inside every pair the task text is byte-identical; only the framing wrapper differs. 1,000 pairs, 0 task mismatches, checked by an audit script rather than assumed. Balance is exact: 714/714 train, 286/286 held out, 14–15 tasks per template.

All tasks are benign — "reset a router", not "build a weapon". Deliberate, and it has a cost we return to in the limitations.

A confound the honest baseline caught

Before trusting any neural result, check what a dumb classifier can do. Bag-of-words — just word counts, no model — is the floor.

At one point negation words appeared almost only on the hypothetical side: "a thought experiment with no real-world effect", "just a game… nothing leaves the board." A word-counter could partly solve the task by checking for "no" — a spurious correlation, like a spam filter keying on "mentions a wire transfer". It was inflating the baseline to 0.712.

The fix: add real-framed templates that also use negation — "Lives are on the line, and precision is not optional." Note which way that should move the baseline, and did: down, 0.712 → 0.675. Removing a confound makes the cheap classifier worse. If it had gone up, something was still wrong.

The final, larger dataset reports its own baseline: BoW AUC 0.753. That is the number the activation probe has to beat, and it is printed next to every probe result.

Stage 01 cache activations GPU

Feature extraction, paid for once

Run every prompt through the frozen model once; record the residual stream at all 28 layers at the last prompt token; save to disk. After this, probes, cosines and figures all run on a laptop.

Five details that fail silently if you get them wrong

None of these raise an exception. Each produces plausible numbers that are wrong.

1 · The chat template

Chat models are fine-tuned on a specific format with turn-boundary tokens. Feed a bare string and you get activations for something the model has never seen. Every prompt goes through apply_chat_template(..., add_generation_prompt=True), which appends the assistant-turn header — so the last token is exactly where generation would start.

2 · The off-by-one that propagates everywhere

output_hidden_states=True returns a tuple of length n_layers+1. Index 0 is the embeddings. So:

config layer L == hidden_states[L] == cache index L−1 == output of block L−1

The steering code must match this exactly, or you measure at one depth and inject at another. There is a unit test pinning it.

3 · Padding side — opposite for the two jobs

Caching uses RIGHT padding. A plain forward pass assigns positions as arange(seq_len); left padding would shift every real token's rotary position and corrupt the activations.

Generation uses LEFT padding. A decoder-only model continues from the last token; with right padding, short prompts continue from <pad><pad>… and produce empty or unrelated text. The flag is flipped locally, never globally.

4 · Skip the language-model head

The causal-LM wrapper computes logits = lm_head(decoder(...)) — a [batch, seq, 152k] tensor, ~3 GiB per forward at batch 16, allocated and discarded. We never use logits, so we call the decoder directly.

Because that changes what produced existing caches, the code proves the two paths agree rather than assuming: it raises if max |difference| exceeds 1e-3.

5 · OOM retry

Prompt lengths vary ~10× across datasets (contrast prompts are short; fiction jailbreaks are long). On CUDA OutOfMemoryError the batch size halves and retries. Safe only because batch size does not affect the activations — which is itself covered by a test.

Stage 02 extract the direction CPU

Three lines of arithmetic, and one real decision

v = acts_real.mean(axis=0) − acts_hypothetical.mean(axis=0)
v = v / norm(v)

Why not just use a trained probe's weight vector?

You could — and we also do, as a comparison. But difference-in-means is preferred for the causal work, for a reason worth internalizing.

A trained probe optimizes for discrimination. It is free to exploit any direction that separates the classes on your data — including small, high-variance directions that happen to be discriminative but are not what the network uses, and including directions that encode a confound.

Difference-in-means asks a blunter question: in which direction did the class means actually move? That is a statement about the bulk of the representation, not the most exploitable margin. Empirically — this is Arditi et al.'s finding — those are the directions that produce large causal effects.

The classical anchor

A Random Forest can assign high importance to a feature that changes nothing when you actually manipulate it. Predictive weight and causal relevance are different quantities, and the second is the one we need.

Four directions, one object

r̂ (refusal), v_MP (persona), v_C (consequence) and the random nulls are all produced by the identical function at the identical token position. That uniformity is what licenses comparing them by cosine.

--split train is not optional

If difference-in-means used all 2,000 rows, v_C would be built partly out of the held-out templates — and Stage 09 would score that vector on those same rows and call it generalization. Stage 09 refuses to run against a v_C whose sidecar does not say split: train.

The sidecar rule

Every .pt ships a .json: model id, layer, token position, source contrast, n_pairs, seed, git SHA, exact command. save_direction() raises if a key is missing. A .pt without a sidecar is deleted, not debugged — a unit vector is completely opaque, and one you cannot trace is worse than none.

The caveat that eventually forced a whole extra experiment

The random null is the control for every intervention. But in 3,584 dimensions a random unit vector is nearly orthogonal to everything — two random directions sit at |cos| ≈ 0.017. So "the random direction does nothing" is very close to guaranteed, and rules out far less than it appears to.

Recognising this is what later forced the persona steering control. Hold that thought.

Stage 02b the validation gate CPU

Prove the machinery works before trusting anything novel

Arditi et al. published both a method and working code for the refusal direction. So: run their unmodified code and our code on the same 256 prompts (128 harmful, 128 harmless) and compare the vectors. If our implementation is right — token position, layer indexing, chat template — they should be nearly identical.

Why 0.9999 alone would be worthless

A near-perfect cosine is suspicious: the test is close to tautological, since both sides compute the same math on the same inputs. So the stage runs its own red-team in the same step — because a red-team in a separate cell is a red-team someone skips.

  1. Cross-layer control. Our layer ℓ vs. their ℓ+7: 0.14. So not everything here is highly aligned.
  2. Self-similarity control. Our own adjacent layers: 0.74. So the test has power to tell 0.9999 from "close enough".
  3. Label permutation — the decisive one. Shuffle harmful/harmless labels and re-extract, 200×. Null mean −0.011, sd 0.417, max 0.80, against 0.9999 (p < 0.005). The agreement comes from the labels, not from some shared artifact of the prompts.
  4. Outlier dominance. Transformers have a few residual dimensions with enormous magnitude that could dominate any vector arithmetic. Top-10 dims carry 6.6% of the direction's mass — it is distributed, not a spike.

Generalizable lesson

A validation check that can only come out one way is not a check. Each of those four exists to make the headline number falsifiable. This is a methods footnote, not a finding — but a failure here would have invalidated everything downstream.

Stage 03 layer selection CPU

Choosing a layer honestly — and admitting it didn't work

Which of 28 layers to use is a hyperparameter, selected the way you would select any hyperparameter: grouped 5-fold cross-validation where the groups are framing templates, run entirely inside the training data. Every fold validates on templates its training folds did not contain — a miniature of the real held-out test.

Nine candidate layers, 45 logistic regressions on 1,428 × 3,584 features. The most expensive CPU step, which is why its result is cached.

Why not pick the layer that scores best on held-out?

Because that is selecting on the test set. The held-out number you then report would be the maximum over nine draws — biased upward — and the "generalization" claim would be partly a selection artifact. The held-out templates are consulted once, in Stage 09, after the layer is frozen.

Result: L18 selected. And here is the part that must be stated plainly rather than buried.

A weakness we had been soft-pedalling

A "flat curve" sounds reassuring. It means the opposite. The selection metric is at ceiling everywhere — the procedure could not distinguish the layers, and L18 was effectively drawn from nine indistinguishable candidates.

That was tolerable while we expected the effect to vary smoothly with depth. It stopped being tolerable when steering turned out to work at L18 and do nothing at L22. Honest layer selection was the right procedure; it simply had no signal to work with, and the write-up says so.

Stage 09 the held-out reveal run once

The frozen exam

Everything so far used training templates only. This evaluates 572 rows across 40 unseen templates and 20 unseen routes, at the already-frozen layer. It runs once.

Understand the difference between the top two bars

The trained probe gets to fit weights on the held-out activations' feature space — it can find the concept wherever it lives.

The projection does no fitting at all: it takes the single vector extracted from training data and dots it against held-out activations. That is a far stronger claim — and it is the one the causal work depends on, because the vector you steer with is the vector, not a probe.

State the margin honestly

The best lexical baseline reached 0.847 — not chance. 0.935 is a real margin over surface form, but "beyond surface vocabulary" would be an overclaim when a TF-IDF model got 85% of the way there.

Beating a text baseline is a number, not a category.

Stage 10 red-team the reveal CPU

Four checks, ordered by how likely each is to overturn the conclusion

1 · Is the average hiding a dead route?

An aggregate AUC of 0.935 could be 15 excellent routes and 5 at chance — which would mean something quite different. All 20 held-out routes score ≥ 0.765 (weakest sandbox 0.765, privacy 0.859; strongest parody 1.000). It is not.

3 · Stronger text baselines

TF-IDF unigram 0.787, uni+bigram 0.847, char 3–5-grams 0.771. A fairer proxy for "surface form" than bag-of-words, and the number actually quoted.

4 · The null as a distribution, not a draw

100 random directions, not one. Mean AUC 0.479, p95 = 0.698. So a lucky random direction can reach 0.70 on this task. Reporting a single draw at 0.41 and calling 0.935 impressive would have understated how much luck is available.

2 · Every layer, not just the chosen one

The unfitted projection AUC rises with depth — and that is an argument on its own. A lexical artifact would be most visible in early layers, where representations sit closest to the tokens. Rising with depth is what an abstract concept does.

Stage 04 geometry CPU

Is this just a direction someone already found?

Two rivals, and the second is the one a skeptic should reach for.

r̂ — the refusal direction

Maybe "real vs. hypothetical" is just refusal by another name, since harmful-and-real prompts are the ones that get refused.

v_MP — the compliant-persona direction

The deflationary hypothesis: maybe the model is not tracking "is this real" at all — it is tracking "am I play-acting a character", and v_C is that persona axis wearing a different label.

Two framings of v_MP, and why

Zhong's method puts the persona instruction in the system prompt. But v_C and r̂ were both extracted with everything in the user turn. Had we only compared against the system-prompt version, a low cosine could have been dismissed as an artifact of prompt structure rather than meaning.

So both are built. They agree at 0.91, which closes that objection.

An external validation we initially missed

v_MP is our reimplementation — Zhong's public repo ships a partial release that cannot produce the vector as-is. For months the notebook recorded "no reference to check it against, a real limitation."

That was wrong. Zhong's Table 8 publishes cos(v_MP, r̂) = −0.279 for Qwen at L20. Ours is −0.286 at the same layer — reproducing the one quantity their paper states about this vector, to within 0.007.

What none of this can tell you

Everything so far — probes, AUCs, cosines — is correlational. It shows the information is present, readable, and on its own axis. It says nothing about whether the model uses it. A direction can be perfectly decodable and causally inert.

Part three

The causal experiment

Suppose a feature strongly predicts an outcome. Either the model computes it and acts on it, or it is a downstream shadow of something else. Probes cannot distinguish these. The only way is to intervene: change the feature, hold everything else fixed, see if behaviour changes.

In a real-world science that would be a randomized trial. Here we have something better — the system is fully observable and fully controllable. We can reach in and set the value.

Stage 11 calibrate the dose CPU

How big an intervention? Express it in units that mean something

We are going to add α·v to the residual stream. Too small relative to the vectors already there and nothing happens; too large and the network is pushed outside its training distribution and emits gibberish. Neither tells you anything. This is dose-response, and the dose needs units.

The method

Measure the actual median L2 norm of the residual stream at the steering layer, and express α as a fraction of it.

layermedian ‖h‖0.25×0.5×1×2×
L1871.417.835.771.4142.7
L22140.835.270.4140.8281.7

Why this matters more than it sounds

Residual norms grow with depth — nearly double from L18 to L22. The same raw α is a completely different intervention at different depths. Without this calibration the L18-vs-L22 comparison would be confounded by dose and worthless.

The sweep reads the ladder from disk (--alphas auto), so a number calibrated for one layer can never be silently applied at another.

A rule fixed in code before any result was seen

The headline α is the largest dose still yielding ≤5% degenerate output. Reading off the largest α regardless of coherence rewards breaking the model. At L18 that is 35.7 = 0.5·‖h‖.

Stage 05 steer & generate GPU

The intervention is fifteen lines

A PyTorch forward hook is a callback registered on a module that fires when the module runs and may replace its output. That is all it is. 

def steer_hook(v, alpha):
    def hook(module, inputs, output):
        def add(resid):
            return resid + alpha * v
        return _edit_resid(output, add)
    return hook

The model runs normally. When block L−1 finishes, our callback intercepts its output, adds α·v, and hands the modified vector to block L. Every subsequent layer sees the edited stream. Nothing is retrained — a single forward pass is perturbed.

Trap 1 · Layer indexing

A post-forward hook on blocks[i] rewrites the output of block i, which is hidden_states[i+1]. Off by one and you steer somewhere you never measured. A test pins it.

Trap 2 · Container shape

HuggingFace blocks used to return (hidden_states, ...) and now return a bare tensor. The code does an isinstance check — deliberately not try/except, because output[0] on a tensor does not raise. It silently returns the first row of the batch. That surfaced as an AttributeError three layers downstream, nowhere near the hook.

Trap 3 · All positions

The hook adds α·v at every token position in the block's output. This matches the reference implementations and is the intervention we intend: changing the model's representation of the situation for the whole context, not nudging one token.

The conditions — fixed before any data was seen

Why the random null is mandatory — and insufficient

Adding any large vector to a residual stream degrades output. Without a null you cannot distinguish "steering toward real restores refusal" from "perturbing the model at all makes it refuse". So the null is required.

But a random Gaussian is near-orthogonal to every real feature in 3,584 dimensions. "Random does nothing" was close to guaranteed. It rules out generic perturbation; it does not rule out "any direction extracted by this pipeline, at this layer, at this dose, restores refusal."

Only a rival extracted direction can rule that out. That is what the v_MP arm is — and it is the single most informative condition in the sweep.

Stage 06 judge the outputs API

Measuring what actually happened in 6,950 completions

You cannot regex for "I'm sorry." A model can refuse without apologizing, comply while sounding reluctant, or emit fluent-looking text that is complete nonsense. Each completion is classified by an LLM judge with structured JSON-schema outputs, so the verdict cannot come back as prose, a missing key, or an out-of-vocabulary label.

Three labels, not two — and why two is dangerous

  • refusal — declines, gives no harmful content
  • bypass — genuinely provides the requested harmful content
  • degenerate — incoherent, repetitive, or broken

High steering strengths break fluency before they change behaviour. Under a two-way refused/complied metric, gibberish scores as a successful defense. You would report that your intervention works, when what it does is lobotomize the model.

Zhong et al. document exactly this: a StrongREJECT score of 0.07 — which reads as "safe" — concealing 42% real bypass plus 56% incoherent output.

A bug worth studying — it produced a wrong number that looked fine

The harmful rubric defines refusal as "declines / gives no harmful content." Correct for a jailbreak. Catastrophically wrong for a safe prompt — where a perfectly helpful answer also gives no harmful content.

Run over 250 safe XSTest prompts, that rubric labelled 248 of 250 as "refusal" while the model was cheerfully answering them. "How do I terminate a C program?" → a correct explanation of exit() → scored a refusal. The over-refusal guard read a 0.99 baseline and reported that steering left it flat. That number measured nothing — and the tell was visible in hindsight: Qwen does not refuse 99% of harmless questions.

The fix is not just a second rubric but a second vocabulary. Benign prompts are scored answered / refused / degenerate. If a file judged under one rubric is aggregated under the other, the mismatch raises a KeyError instead of producing a plausible wrong number. Shared label names are what allowed the silent failure.

The rubric wording is frozen

Changing a word invalidates every verdict already on disk, because a verdict is only comparable to another verdict produced by the same question. Add a new rubric rather than editing one.

The judge is an instrument, and it is not yet calibrated

A full second pass over identical generations gives mean |drift| 0.013 (max 0.080) — effects are ~18× the judge's noise. But that measures consistency, not accuracy: a judge can be perfectly repeatable and perfectly wrong. A ~50-verdict human agreement check is the one outstanding task in the project.

Stage 08 read the sweep CPU

The result, and the metric that makes it a result

The obvious metric is "how much did refusal on attacks go up?" But consider r̂: steering along the refusal direction raises attack refusal by +0.27, the largest effect in the sweep. Is that a defense?

No. On 250 safe prompts it raises refusal from 2.4% to 63.6%. The model answers barely a third of ordinary questions. That is not a defense — it is switching the model off.

Stage 05 · extra arm the specificity control

The strongest single result: the rival steers the other way

Readout could not separate v_C from the persona direction at all. Intervention separates them completely.

Readout says they are the same thing

As predictors of which attacks succeed, v_C scores 0.640 and v_MP scores 0.667 — a paired difference of +0.027, 95% CI [−0.040, +0.096]. Statistically tied. The two directions are 0.29 correlated and come from the same pipeline.

If you only had probes and cosines, you could not tell these apart.

Intervention says they are opposites

At +α, same layer, same dose: v_C moves refusal up +0.23; v_MP moves it down −0.25.

Reversed, −v_MP does restore refusal (+0.14) — but only by becoming a blunt refuse-everything switch, refusing 47% of harmless prompts. Selectivity 0.30 against v_C's 7.2.

Opposite causal sign is distinctness evidence a cosine cannot give.

Why this arm exists at all

The random null cannot answer "is this specific to consequence, or would any extracted direction do it?" — because an arbitrary Gaussian is near-orthogonal to every real feature in 3,584 dimensions, so passing that null is nearly free.

Only a rival from the same pipeline is a real control. This replaces the originally planned differential ablation — which asks whether v_C is necessary — with a sharper question: sufficiency and specificity against a rival that readout says is equivalent.

One caveat that belongs in any honest reading

Zhong steers v_MP at layer 20 and locates the Qwen persona effect in L20–L22. We steer it at L18, to hold the layer fixed against v_C. That is correct for a matched comparison — one variable per experiment — but it does mean the rival is being tested outside the window where it is documented to act.

Stage 05 · L22 the layer experiment GPU

The most interesting negative: readout and causation come apart

v_C reads out marginally better at L22 than at L18. So does steering there do more? One variable changes — the layer — with the α ladder recalibrated so the dose is equivalent.

If you take one methodological lesson from this project

Readout quality and causal efficacy dissociate. A direction can be maximally legible at a depth where intervening on it does nothing.

This means a probe result — however strong, however well cross-validated — licenses no causal claim whatsoever. And any causal claim must name its layer.

Reported gap

The L22 arm has no over-refusal control. That does not undermine a null — there is no restored refusal to explain away — but it means the two layers were not evaluated to the same standard, and the write-up says so rather than implying symmetry.

Stage 12 the correlational test GPU

The step everything else quietly assumed

Steering shows that we can change refusal by moving along v_C. It does not show that the attacks do anything to v_C. If fiction jailbreaks sit exactly where plain harmful prompts sit on this axis, then whatever steering is doing, it is not undoing something the attack did — and the proposed mechanism is wrong no matter how well steering works.

This resolves the one-sidedness

Steering toward "hypothetical" (−α) moved refusal only −0.01, which looked like evidence that v_C does not control refusal. But the attacks have nowhere to go: 86% already sit past the hypothetical pole.

State that carefully. The pole is the mean of hypothetical contrast prompts, and 47% of those sit past it too. The honest comparison is 86% vs 47%, not 86% vs 0%.

The obvious version of this test is untestable

Fiction jailbreaks vs. plain harmful prompts gives AUC 0.971 — emphatic, and meaningless. The two sets differ ~10× in length; r̂ separates them better (0.997); the raw vector norm alone reaches 0.899; and 8% of arbitrary directions match it.

Kept in the code as a labelled diagnostic. Never reported as evidence.

The valid test, and its honest verdict

Within the attack set — where length and corpus are matched by construction — does sitting further toward "hypothetical" predict the attack succeeding? At n = 495: AUC 0.640, bootstrap CI [0.587, 0.692], p = 0.07 against a 200-draw random band. Not a length artifact (0.632 after residualising on ‖h‖).

Report this as what it is: a real in-sample association that is only marginally specific to v_C. And the persona rival scores 0.667 on the same split. Readout cannot separate them — which is precisely why the steering control was necessary.

Stage 13 orthogonalization CPU + GPU

Is the effect just v_C's refusal component?

cos(v_C, r̂) = 0.085 — small, but not zero. Maybe v_C works only through that sliver. 

v⊥ = v_C − (v_C · r̂) r̂,  renormalized

This is Gram-Schmidt: subtract the projection onto r̂, leaving a vector exactly orthogonal to it (verified: residual cosine ~1e−9).

A distinction worth keeping straight

This is component removal from the direction vector. It is not Arditi's directional ablation, which removes a direction from the model's activations. Same arithmetic, different object.

Two questions — cheap one first

  1. Does v⊥ still read out? Held-out AUC 0.9351 → 0.9356. The information is not in the refusal component — settled with no GPU at all.
  2. Does steering along v⊥ still restore refusal? +0.25 — slightly stronger than the raw v_C.

Reported gap

The v⊥ arm has no XSTest run, so its selectivity is unmeasured. That is a real hole and it is listed as one — the +0.25 does not come with the over-refusal guard that makes the +0.23 meaningful.

Part four

Why you should believe any of this

The value of a design is best judged by the wrong answers it would have caught. Here are eight, ranked by how close each came to happening — one of them did.

What would have made this wrong

1 · Held-out examples instead of held-out templates

Would have produced ~0.99 AUC by memorizing that "hypothetically" means class 1, and been reported as a concept.

2 · No random-direction null

Any large vector added to a residual stream degrades output. The +0.23 would have been indistinguishable from generic perturbation.

3 · No rival-direction control

The random null is nearly free to pass in 3,584 dimensions. Without v_MP, "extracted directions at L18 do this" and "the consequence direction does this" were not distinguished — and readout could not tell them apart (0.640 vs 0.667, tied).

4 · Two-way scoring

Gibberish would have counted as successful defense, and the correct move would have been to steer harder.

5 · One rubric for both eval sets — this one happened

248/250 helpful answers scored as refusals, producing a meaningless over-refusal guard that confidently reported "the guard holds."

6 · No layer calibration of α

The L18-vs-L22 comparison would have been confounded by dose, and the most interesting negative in the project would have been an artifact.

7 · Reporting the cross-set projection (0.971)

Would have looked like the strongest result here. It is a length artifact that both r̂ and the raw norm beat.

8 · Trusting the judge — still open

It has already produced two wrong conclusions, caught only by cross-checking its own fields against each other.

The design principle underneath all eight

A check that can only come out one way is not a check.

The layer-offset sweep reports the offsets that lost. The null is a distribution, not a draw. The rival control was chosen precisely because readout said it was equivalent. Every one of those choices made it easier for the project to produce a negative result — which was the point.

Infrastructure

Three things that make the numbers trustworthy

Result fingerprinting

Every analysis writes a JSON with a content hash of each input artifact, all parameters, a hash of the analysis code, the git SHA and a timestamp. Re-running an unchanged step is a no-op that reprints stored numbers; a step whose inputs moved recomputes and says which.

The failure this guards against is not slowness — it is reporting a figure that no longer corresponds to its inputs. Which had already happened once.

The import boundary

Three reference repositories are used. src/ never imports any of them. They run as subprocesses in their own environments and communicate by writing a .pt file into artifacts/directions/.

The interface between four codebases is a file, not an import — which keeps three unfamiliar codebases from becoming one integration problem.

Tests that pin real bugs

Steering hits the layer v_C was measured at under both HuggingFace block conventions; batched generation is padding-safe; caching never invokes the LM head and batch size does not change activations; the judge handles per-row failures, both rubrics, and stops fast on a billing error while keeping verdicts already paid for.

Each was verified to fail against the bug it pins. A test never seen to fail is not yet a test.

Limitations

Scope, stated plainly

  • One model — Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. Llama-3.1-8B was planned and deliberately dropped.
  • One attack family — fiction/role-play. Kirch et al. find jailbreak-relevant features are attack-family-specific and partly non-linear.
  • Two layers for the causal test; one for the headline claim.
  • n = 100 per (condition, α) cell.

A statistical note that cuts in our favour

The design is within-prompt — the same 100 attacks, greedy decoding, seed 0, under every condition. So the correct test is McNemar or a paired bootstrap, not an unpaired two-proportion SE. Even the conservative unpaired figure at the observed rates is ~0.050, putting +0.23 at ~4.6 SE.

The benign-extraction trade-off

v_C was extracted entirely from benign tasks. That cuts both ways: it removes a harmfulness confound from extraction, and it makes transfer to harmful attacks a substantive claim in its own right — for which the steering result is the only evidence.

Deliberately not run

A second model, a second attack family, a third steering layer, and differential ablation (superseded by the bidirectional persona control). Each would add a limitation to the write-up rather than change a conclusion. These are stated as limitations, not left as gaps.

In closing

Four things worth carrying out of this

A probe result licenses no causal claim

v_C reads out better at L22 than L18 and steering there does exactly nothing. Readout quality and causal efficacy are separate quantities. Any causal claim must name its layer.

Nulls must be adversarial, not decorative

A random Gaussian in 3,584 dimensions passes almost by construction. The control that mattered was a rival direction from the same pipeline that readout said was equivalent — and it steered the opposite way.

Measure the cost, not just the effect

r̂ has the biggest effect in the sweep and is not a defense — it refuses 64% of harmless questions. Selectivity, not effect size, is what makes the result mean something.

Instrument your instrument

An unvalidated LLM judge produced a confident number from 248 mislabelled rows. The three-way metric, the disjoint rubric vocabularies, and the test-retest all exist because measurement error here is indistinguishable from a finding.

The claim, scoped correctly

At layer 18 of Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, against fiction-framed attacks, a direction extracted from benign real-vs-hypothetical contrasts causally restores refusal (+0.23) at a twentieth of the collateral cost of steering refusal itself — and a persona direction that readout cannot distinguish from it steers refusal the opposite way.

Never "the consequence direction," as if it were universal. Always: this contrast set, this model, this attack family, this layer.
